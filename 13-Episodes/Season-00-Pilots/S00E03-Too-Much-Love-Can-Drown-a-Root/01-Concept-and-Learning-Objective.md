# S00E03 --- Too Much Love Can Drown a Root

## 01 --- Concept & Learning Objective

> **Care means checking first.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Core Mystery

Sprout has been watering a garden plant faithfully, every day, out of
genuine care. But the plant looks droopy and yellowed anyway. If she's
doing everything right, why does it still look unwell?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Central Question

**Is the plant thirsty --- or is something else going on?**

The episode should avoid the easy assumption ("it needs even more
water"). The audience should learn that a wilting plant is a symptom,
not a diagnosis --- the soil itself has to be checked before deciding
what it needs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Scientific Focus

**Overwatering and root oxygen need.**

Roots don't just absorb water --- they also need oxygen, which normally
reaches them through small air pockets in the soil. When soil is kept
constantly saturated, water fills those pockets and pushes the air
out. Roots deprived of oxygen for too long become stressed and can't
take up water or nutrients properly, even though the soil around them
is wet. The visible symptom (drooping, yellowing leaves) looks
identical to underwatering, which is exactly why it's commonly
misdiagnosed and "corrected" with even more water --- making the
problem worse.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# What the Episode Must NOT Claim

- that watering is bad, or that plants don't need water;
- that every droopy/yellow plant is overwatered (underwatering causes
  similar visible symptoms --- this episode is specifically about
  *this* plant, established as sitting in visibly saturated soil);
- that there's a universal watering schedule that works for every
  plant (the point is to check the specific plant's soil, not follow
  a fixed rule);
- that the plant will definitely recover (the episode ends with a
  corrected care approach, not a guaranteed cure).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Observable Evidence

- the plant's leaves are drooping and yellowed;
- the soil is visibly dark and damp, not dry or cracked;
- pressing into the soil, it feels saturated/waterlogged --- soft and
  squelchy rather than moist-and-crumbly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Sprout's Key Discovery

Sprout (with Brooks' guidance) checks the soil itself instead of just
looking at the plant. She expected dry soil (which would explain
wilting from thirst) and instead finds the opposite --- soil that's
already saturated. This reversal is the discovery that reframes the
question.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Brooks' Explanation (age-appropriate)

> Roots don't just drink water --- they breathe through the tiny spaces
> around them. Too much water fills up that space, and there's no air
> left for the roots at all.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Care Message

**Check the soil before you water.**

A simple finger test (a couple of centimetres down) tells you whether
a plant actually needs water, rather than watering on a fixed habit or
schedule. The episode frames this as attentiveness, not restriction ---
"real care checks first," not "water less."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Child-Level Mental Model

**Roots need to breathe too --- check before you water, don't just water
because you love it.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Discovery Frame Candidate

A close-up cross-section-style inset showing water filling the spaces
around a root instead of air --- simple, not overly technical, paired
visually with a "healthy moist soil" comparison if space allows.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Badge

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Discovery Arc

```text
OBSERVE   — Sprout waters faithfully; the plant still looks unwell
QUESTION  — why, if she's doing everything right?
INVESTIGATE — check the soil itself, not just the plant
EVIDENCE  — the soil is already saturated, not dry
DISCOVER  — it's not thirsty — it's drowning
UNDERSTAND — roots need air pockets in the soil too
CARE      — check the soil before watering, every time
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Relationship Test

Sprout's love and diligence are never mocked --- her instinct to care is
correct, her method needs refining, and Brooks is careful to frame the
correction that way (see Panel 5's line: "You can" love a plant too
much, validating her question rather than dismissing it).
