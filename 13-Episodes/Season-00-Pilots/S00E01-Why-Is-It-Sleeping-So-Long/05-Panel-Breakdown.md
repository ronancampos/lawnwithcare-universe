# S00E01 --- Why Is It Sleeping So Long?

## 05 --- Panel Breakdown

## Global Production Standard

Each panel is produced as a separate image.

-   Master size: **1080 × 1350 px**
-   Aspect ratio: **4:5 portrait**
-   One scene per file
-   No multi-panel AI-generated comic pages
-   Final dialogue added after artwork approval
-   All panels must remain individually usable in an Instagram carousel

Final filenames:

``` text
S00E01-P01.png
S00E01-P02.png
S00E01-P03.png
S00E01-P04.png
S00E01-P05.png
S00E01-P06.png
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Global Continuity Lock

All six images depict one continuous early-spring event in the same
residential yard.

## Sprout

Lock to approved Sprout references:

-   approximately 9 years old;
-   golden curly blonde hair;
-   light-green eyes;
-   freckles;
-   green headband;
-   cream field shirt;
-   olive-green shorts;
-   brown outdoor boots;
-   Discovery Band;
-   magnifying loupe;
-   canonical proportions.

## Brooks

Lock to approved Brooks references:

-   canonical face and proportions;
-   campaign hat;
-   canonical forest-green ranger outfit;
-   approved chest insignia/badge configuration;
-   dark outdoor boots;
-   notebook and pencil where appropriate;
-   approximately 188 cm tall.

Do not ask image generation to reconstruct the official Lawn With Care
logo.

## Relative Scale

Sprout: approximately 130--135 cm.

Brooks: approximately 188 cm.

Maintain the relationship reference even when perspective changes.

## The Shrub

Use one consistent hardy evergreen shrub throughout the episode.

Lock:

-   approximate height;
-   silhouette;
-   branch architecture;
-   damaged zones;
-   position in yard;
-   key bud-bearing branch;
-   relationship to nearby hardscape/background.

The shrub should show localized brown/tan winter-damaged foliage,
especially on more exposed portions, while retaining a subtle viable bud
that becomes important later.

## Weather and Light

Lock:

-   early spring;
-   cool but pleasant conditions;
-   same general time of day;
-   soft natural daylight;
-   compatible light direction;
-   no abrupt weather changes.

The yard is transitioning out of winter, not fully lush summer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Text-Safe Rule

Artwork should be generated clean, without final speech bubbles or
dialogue.

Reserve useful negative space in every composition.

Keep critical faces, hands, loupe, bud and scientific evidence away from
crop-sensitive edges.

Do not allow planned dialogue areas to cover the central clue.

# PANEL 01 --- Spring Is Waking Up

## File

`S00E01-P01.png`

## Function

ESTABLISH / WONDER

## Camera

Medium-wide environmental shot.

Child-friendly eye level, slightly low enough to make the yard feel
explorable.

## Composition

Sprout occupies the foreground or near-middle ground, crouched beside a
small sign of fresh spring growth.

Brooks appears farther back or off to one side.

The evergreen shrub that will become the mystery may be subtly visible
elsewhere in the frame, but it should not yet dominate.

## Sprout Pose

-   crouched or kneeling naturally;
-   leaning toward emerging growth;
-   delighted expression;
-   one hand near the ground;
-   loupe visible or held casually.

## Brooks Pose

-   relaxed standing/walking position;
-   watching Sprout rather than leading her;
-   notebook may be present;
-   secondary visual weight.

## Environment

Show early-spring transition:

-   muted lawn;
-   damp soil;
-   small fresh growth;
-   dormant vegetation;
-   restrained remaining winter cues if appropriate.

Avoid a fully green summer garden.

## Emotional Read

"WOW --- spring is coming back."

## Dialogue

Sprout:

"Brooks! Look! Everything's waking up!"

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Prefer upper-left or upper-center negative space, depending on character
placement.

Do not place Brooks' face in the planned bubble zone.

## Continuity Purpose

Establish:

-   clothing;
-   lighting;
-   yard;
-   season;
-   character scale;
-   initial spatial relationship.

## Avoid

-   lush summer lawn;
-   giant flowers;
-   Brooks pointing out the discovery;
-   heavy branding;
-   speech text rendered by AI.

# PANEL 02 --- The Exception

## File

`S00E01-P02.png`

## Function

MYSTERY / CONCERN

## Camera

Medium shot.

Closer than P01.

## Composition

Sprout is now near the evergreen shrub.

The shrub occupies a substantial part of the image.

Brown/tan exposed foliage must be clearly readable.

Brooks may be approaching or visible behind her, but Sprout and the
plant are the visual focus.

## Sprout Pose

-   standing, crouching or leaning toward shrub;
-   puzzled expression;
-   hand slightly raised toward damaged foliage without unnecessarily
    pulling it;
-   attention fully on the plant.

## Brooks Pose

-   approaching calmly or stopping beside her;
-   no pointing at the answer.

## Shrub

The damaged portion should look concerning but botanically plausible.

Do not make the entire shrub a featureless dead brown mass.

Preserve branch/bud structure needed later.

## Emotional Read

"Something doesn't match."

## Dialogue

Sprout:

"Except this one..."

"Why is it sleeping so long?"

Optional if space remains:

"Is it dead?"

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Prefer upper-right if Sprout occupies left/lower-left.

Keep foliage evidence visible.

## Avoid

-   horror-like dead plant;
-   disease spots;
-   insects;
-   animal chewing;
-   broken trunk;
-   obvious mechanical damage;
-   Brooks already examining the bud.

# PANEL 03 --- What Do You Notice?

## File

`S00E01-P03.png`

## Function

QUESTION / INVESTIGATION SETUP

## Camera

Intimate two-shot.

Medium or medium-close.

## Composition

Brooks crouches or kneels beside Sprout.

They share the same investigation space.

The shrub remains visible beside them.

Brooks' height difference should still be understandable even though he
has lowered himself.

## Brooks Pose

-   crouched/kneeling;
-   calm open posture;
-   notebook may rest in one hand;
-   free hand may gesture toward the plant generally;
-   do not point directly at the viable bud.

## Sprout Pose

-   focused;
-   looking between Brooks and plant;
-   beginning to compare areas;
-   loupe ready.

## Eye Lines

Brooks may first look at Sprout while asking the question.

Sprout's attention then returns to the shrub.

## Dialogue

Brooks:

"Maybe. But let's not guess yet."

"What do you notice?"

Sprout:

"The outside looks brown and dry."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Use upper zone split into clean conversational spaces.

Avoid stacking large bubbles over faces.

## Relationship Canon Requirement

This panel must communicate respect, not adult authority.

Brooks is physically closer to Sprout's level.

## Avoid

-   Brooks standing over Sprout;
-   lecture pose;
-   finger wagging;
-   Brooks holding the loupe instead of Sprout;
-   Brooks revealing the clue.

# PANEL 04 --- Look Closer

## File

`S00E01-P04.png`

## Function

INVESTIGATION / FOCUS

## Camera

Close or medium-close observation shot.

This is the closest investigative image so far.

## Composition

Sprout dominates the composition.

Her loupe is positioned near the key bud-bearing branch.

Brooks remains visible but secondary, ideally behind or beside her.

The key scientific clue must not be hidden.

## Sprout Pose

-   concentrated expression;
-   loupe held carefully;
-   body leaning toward plant;
-   natural child posture;
-   no exaggerated detective pose.

## Brooks Pose

-   observing Sprout's process;
-   patient;
-   hands/notebook not interfering.

## Discovery Frame

Primary candidate.

A controlled inset or visual magnification may show what Sprout sees:

-   damaged brown/tan foliage;
-   a subtle viable bud.

The Discovery Frame must not resemble a futuristic HUD.

It should feel organic, educational and consistent with the franchise.

## Dialogue

Sprout:

"Hmm..."

"Wait..."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Minimal text.

Preserve maximum visual room for investigation.

## Visual Priority

LOUPE → BUD AREA → SPROUT'S EYES/REACTION.

## Avoid

-   giant glowing bud;
-   magical effects;
-   futuristic scanner graphics;
-   Brooks pointing at the bud;
-   oversized loupe obscuring Sprout's face;
-   final answer text.

# PANEL 05 --- This Bud Is Alive!

## File

`S00E01-P05.png`

## Function

DISCOVERY / SURPRISE

## Camera

Close-medium reaction/discovery shot.

## Composition

Sprout and the bud-bearing branch share primary importance.

The viable bud should be readable without becoming botanically absurd.

Brooks reacts to Sprout rather than taking over.

## Sprout Pose

-   delighted realization;
-   loupe still present;
-   gesture toward the discovered bud;
-   confident, excited expression.

## Brooks Pose

-   leaning in slightly;
-   warm approving reaction;
-   may smile;
-   notebook remains secondary.

## Dialogue

Sprout:

"Brooks! This bud is still alive!"

Brooks:

"Good eye, Sprout."

Optional:

"So what does that tell us?"

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Give Sprout the dominant bubble space.

Brooks receives a smaller secondary area.

## Scientific Visual Requirement

The image must still show surrounding damaged foliage.

Do not depict the entire branch suddenly green.

## Emotional Read

"We found evidence!"

## Avoid

-   magical glow;
-   plant suddenly healed;
-   new leaves exploding from the bud;
-   Brooks holding or discovering the bud first;
-   celebration so exaggerated that the scientific clue becomes
    secondary.

# PANEL 06 --- Look Before You Decide

## File

`S00E01-P06.png`

## Function

UNDERSTAND / CARE / CLOSE

## Camera

Medium or medium-wide closing shot.

Pull back slightly after the close investigation.

## Composition

Brooks and Sprout are beside the same shrub.

Both characters and the plant are clearly visible.

The damaged foliage remains visible.

The scene should feel calmer than P05.

## Brooks Pose

-   near Sprout rather than towering;
-   gentle explanatory gesture;
-   notebook may be closed or relaxed;
-   calm expression.

## Sprout Pose

-   thoughtful;
-   looking at shrub with new understanding;
-   loupe relaxed;
-   active rather than passive.

## Dialogue

Brooks:

"Winter can dry out exposed parts, even when some of the plant is still
alive."

Sprout:

"So we shouldn't give up on it yet."

Brooks:

"Exactly. Let's watch what grows before we decide what to cut."

## Closing Caption

"LOOK CLOSER BEFORE YOU DECIDE."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

This panel needs the largest text allowance.

Reserve a clean upper region and/or separated left/right spaces.

Do not reduce characters to tiny figures merely to make room for text.

If layout becomes crowded, dialogue must be shortened during production
review.

## Scientific Visual Requirement

Brown damaged foliage remains brown.

No instant recovery.

## Emotional Read

"Now we understand enough to make a more careful choice."

## Avoid

-   pruning action already happening;
-   removed shrub;
-   fully recovered plant;
-   giant educational text block;
-   Brooks lecturing from a standing authority pose.

# Carousel Visual Progression

The six images should feel like one visual journey:

``` text
P01  WIDE       — discover the season
P02  MEDIUM     — discover the mystery
P03  TWO-SHOT   — discover the method
P04  CLOSE      — discover the evidence
P05  CLOSE-MED  — understand the clue
P06  MED-WIDE   — understand the meaning
```

The camera metaphor is intentional:

> **Understanding grows as we look closer.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Cross-Panel Position Logic

Exact blocking may adapt to composition, but the yard should maintain
understandable geography.

Recommended continuity:

-   shrub stays in the same yard location;
-   major background objects do not jump sides arbitrarily;
-   light direction remains stable;
-   damage pattern stays stable;
-   characters do not change clothing;
-   loupe does not change design;
-   Brooks' notebook does not randomly change form.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Panel Independence Test

Every panel must pass two tests:

## Carousel Test

Does it connect naturally to the image before and after it?

## Standalone Test

If someone sees only this image, is there still one clear visual idea?

Both matter for Instagram.
