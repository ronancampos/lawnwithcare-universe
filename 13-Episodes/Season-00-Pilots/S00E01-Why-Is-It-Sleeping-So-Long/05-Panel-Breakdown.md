# S00E01 --- Why Is It Sleeping So Long?

## 05 --- Panel Breakdown

## Global Production Standard

Each panel is produced as a separate image.

-   Master size: **1080 × 1350 px**
-   Aspect ratio: **4:5 portrait**
-   One scene per file
-   No multi-panel AI-generated comic pages
-   Dialogue from `04-Script.md` is included in the first-draft
    generation prompt for every panel (see `06-Visual-Prompts.md`);
    a controlled correction pass remains available before export
-   All panels must remain individually usable in an Instagram
    carousel

Final filenames:

``` text
S00E01-P01.png
S00E01-P02.png
S00E01-P03.png
S00E01-P04.png
S00E01-P05.png
S00E01-P06.png
S00E01-P07.png
S00E01-P08.png
```

# Global Continuity Lock

All eight images depict one continuous early-spring morning in the
same residential yard.

## Sprout

Lock to approved Sprout references: approximately 9 years old; golden
curly blonde hair; light-green eyes; freckles; green headband; cream
field shirt; olive-green shorts; brown outdoor boots; Discovery Band;
magnifying loupe; canonical proportions.

## Brooks

Lock to approved Brooks references: canonical face and proportions
(bald under the hat --- no visible hairstyle when the hat is worn, per
the Brooks Visual Canon Audit); campaign hat; canonical forest-green
ranger outfit; approved chest insignia/badge configuration; dark
outdoor boots; notebook and pencil where appropriate; approximately
188 cm tall.

Do not ask image generation to reconstruct the official Lawn With Care
logo.

## Relative Scale

Sprout: approximately 130--135 cm. Brooks: approximately 188 cm.
Maintain the relationship reference even when perspective changes.

## The Yard --- Master Environment Lock

This revision leans harder on environment detail than v1.0, per
production direction, so the yard itself needs a locked "set" the
same way the characters do. Establish once (ideally via a dedicated
environment reference image, as in v1.0) and repeat consistently:

-   **Layout:** a modest residential backyard, fenced on at least two
    visible sides with a simple wooden board fence; a stepping-stone
    path running roughly diagonally through the yard; a section of
    the house's siding visible at one edge of wide shots.
-   **The shrub:** positioned along the fence line, roughly a third of
    the way into the yard from the house, reachable from the path
    without leaving it.
-   **Ground plane:** muted dormant lawn with faint early green
    re-emerging in patches, especially near the path; damp,
    dark, recently-thawed soil visible along garden/bed edges; a few
    flattened, weathered leaves left over from fall, concentrated near
    the shrub's base and fence line.
-   **Leftover snow:** small patches only, tucked into shaded
    corners --- the north side of the shrub, the base of the fence in
    permanent shade. Never a snow-covered yard. Once placed (in P01),
    these patches should not spread, vanish, or relocate across the
    other seven panels.
-   **Secondary vegetation:** one or two bare deciduous saplings near
    the fence with faint bud swelling; low, still-dormant perennial
    beds along the house side.
-   **Sky and light:** soft, pale, slightly overcast early-morning sky
    with thin horizontal cloud bands; light direction fixed from one
    side (art director's choice, but locked once chosen) producing
    long, soft, cool shadows consistent with early morning / early
    spring, never a high summer-noon sun.

## The Shrub

Use one consistent hardy evergreen shrub throughout the episode. Lock:
approximate height; silhouette; branch architecture; damaged zones;
position in yard; key bud-bearing branch; relationship to the fence
and path. The shrub should show localized brown/tan winter-damaged
foliage, especially on more exposed portions, while retaining a
subtle viable bud that becomes important later, and (from Panel 07
onward) a visibly healthier, more sheltered side that makes the
"some tissue damaged, other tissue alive" idea legible without
dialogue.

# Text-Safe Rule

Because dialogue is now baked into the first-draft prompt, negative
space still matters --- it protects the correction pass, not just the
first pass. Keep critical faces, hands, loupe, bud and scientific
evidence away from crop-sensitive edges. Do not allow dialogue areas
to cover the central clue, even in the initial draft.

# PANEL 01 --- The Yard Wakes Up

## File

`S00E01-P01.png`

## Function

ESTABLISH ENVIRONMENT

## Camera

Wide environmental shot. Slightly elevated from true ground level --
just enough to read the whole yard's layout -- but still feels
human-scale, not aerial.

## Composition (background → midground → foreground)

-   **Background:** house siding at one edge, soft overcast sky, thin
    cloud bands, fence line running across the upper-middle third of
    the frame.
-   **Midground:** the mystery shrub along the fence, readable but
    small -- not yet the subject; one or two bare saplings; the
    stepping-stone path curving through the space.
-   **Foreground:** muted lawn transitioning out of dormancy, a patch
    of leftover snow tucked in a shaded corner, damp soil along a bed
    edge.

## Characters

Optional. If included, Brooks and/or Sprout are small figures
entering from the path or a corner of the frame -- unposed,
non-expressive, purely for scale and continuity setup. This panel's
job is the yard, not the characters.

## Emotional Read

"Winter is loosening its grip. Spring hasn't arrived yet."

## Dialogue

CAPTION (optional): *"Early spring in the yard."*

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Lower third, if used -- keep the upper two-thirds clear for the
environment to breathe.

## Continuity Purpose

Establishes: yard layout, fence, path, house edge, shrub position,
leftover-snow placement, light direction, season. Every later panel
inherits these choices.

## Avoid

-   a fully green, lush, or snow-covered yard;
-   the shrub already dominating the frame;
-   character posing/expression that anticipates a beat that hasn't
    happened yet;
-   AI-rendered logos.

# PANEL 02 --- Something Is Growing

## File

`S00E01-P02.png`

## Function

WONDER

## Camera

Medium shot, child-friendly eye level, noticeably closer than P01.

## Composition

Sprout occupies the foreground/near-middle ground, crouched beside a
small, specific sign of spring -- fresh green shoots or an emerging
bulb pushing through damp soil. Brooks appears farther back or to one
side, secondary. The shrub from P01 is visible, softly out of focus,
maintaining spatial continuity without becoming the subject yet.

## Sprout Pose

Crouched naturally, leaning toward the new growth, one hand near the
ground, delighted expression, loupe visible but not necessarily in
use.

## Brooks Pose

Relaxed standing/walking position, watching Sprout rather than
leading her, notebook may be present.

## Scenery Detail

Dark, damp soil texture immediately around the new growth; a few
flattened, weathered fall leaves nearby; the same fence line and
shrub from P01 recognizable in the background.

## Dialogue

SPROUT: "Brooks! Look! Everything's waking up!"

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Upper-left or upper-center, depending on character placement. Keep
Brooks' face out of the bubble zone.

## Continuity Purpose

Confirms clothing, lighting, character scale, and the yard's spatial
layout established in P01, now at closer range.

## Avoid

-   a lush summer lawn;
-   giant flowers;
-   Brooks pointing out the discovery;
-   heavy branding.

# PANEL 03 --- The Exception

## File

`S00E01-P03.png`

## Function

MYSTERY / CONCERN

## Camera

Medium shot, closer than P02.

## Composition

Sprout is now near the evergreen shrub, which occupies a substantial
part of the image. Brown/tan exposed foliage must be clearly
readable, concentrated on the more sun-and-wind-exposed side of the
plant. Brooks may be approaching or visible behind her, but Sprout
and the plant are the visual focus.

## Sprout Pose

Standing, crouching, or leaning toward the shrub, puzzled expression,
hand slightly raised toward the damaged foliage without pulling or
breaking anything.

## Brooks Pose

Approaching calmly or stopping beside her, no pointing at the answer.

## Scenery Detail

The damaged portion should look concerning but botanically
plausible -- do not make the entire shrub a featureless dead brown
mass. Preserve the branch/bud structure needed for later panels.
Mulch or bare soil at the shrub's base, consistent with P01/P02.

## Dialogue

SPROUT: "Except this one..."

SPROUT: "Why is it sleeping so long?"

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Upper-right if Sprout occupies left/lower-left. Keep the damaged
foliage visible.

## Avoid

-   horror-like dead plant;
-   disease spots, insects, or animal chewing;
-   broken trunk or obvious mechanical damage;
-   Brooks already examining the bud.

# PANEL 04 --- What Do You Notice?

## File

`S00E01-P04.png`

## Function

QUESTION / INVESTIGATION SETUP

## Camera

Intimate two-shot, medium or medium-close.

## Composition

Brooks crouches or kneels beside Sprout. They share the same
investigation space. The shrub remains visible beside them; the
wider yard recedes out of focus. Brooks' height difference should
still read even though he has lowered himself.

## Brooks Pose

Crouched/kneeling, calm open posture, notebook may rest in one hand,
free hand may gesture generally toward the plant -- never directly at
the viable bud.

## Sprout Pose

Focused, looking between Brooks and the plant, beginning to compare
areas, loupe ready.

## Eye Lines

Brooks may first look at Sprout while asking the question; Sprout's
attention then returns to the shrub.

## Dialogue

BROOKS: "Maybe. But let's not guess yet."

BROOKS: "What do you notice?"

SPROUT: "The outside looks brown and dry."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Upper zone split into clean conversational spaces -- Brooks upper-left
or upper-center, Sprout's shorter response in a smaller secondary
area. Avoid stacking large bubbles over faces.

## Relationship Canon Requirement

Must communicate respect, not adult authority. Brooks is physically
closer to Sprout's level.

## Avoid

-   Brooks standing over Sprout;
-   lecture pose or finger-wagging;
-   Brooks holding the loupe instead of Sprout;
-   Brooks revealing the clue.

# PANEL 05 --- Look Closer

## File

`S00E01-P05.png`

## Function

INVESTIGATION / FOCUS

## Camera

Close or medium-close observation shot -- the closest investigative
image so far.

## Composition

Sprout dominates the composition. Her loupe is positioned near the
key bud-bearing branch. Brooks remains visible but secondary, ideally
behind or beside her. The key scientific clue must not be hidden.
Background yard elements recede almost entirely -- this panel is about
one branch.

## Sprout Pose

Concentrated expression, loupe held carefully, body leaning toward
the plant, natural child posture, no exaggerated detective pose.

## Brooks Pose

Observing Sprout's process, patient, hands/notebook not interfering.

## Discovery Frame

Primary candidate. A controlled inset or visual magnification shows
what Sprout sees: damaged brown/tan foliage and a subtle viable bud.
Organic and educational, not a HUD, scanner, or futuristic interface.

## Dialogue

SPROUT: "Hmm..."

SPROUT: "Wait..."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Minimal -- preserve maximum visual room for the investigation.

## Visual Priority

LOUPE → BUD AREA → SPROUT'S EYES/REACTION.

## Avoid

-   giant glowing bud, magical effects, futuristic scanner graphics;
-   Brooks pointing at the bud;
-   oversized loupe obscuring Sprout's face;
-   final-answer text appearing early.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# PANEL 06 --- This Bud Is Alive!

## File

`S00E01-P06.png`

## Function

DISCOVERY / SURPRISE

## Camera

Close-medium reaction/discovery shot.

## Composition

Sprout and the bud-bearing branch share primary importance. The
viable bud should be readable without becoming botanically absurd.
Brooks reacts to Sprout rather than taking over.

## Sprout Pose

Delighted realization, loupe still present, gesture toward the
discovered bud, confident and excited expression.

## Brooks Pose

Leaning in slightly, warm approving reaction, may smile, notebook
remains secondary.

## Dialogue

SPROUT: "Brooks! This bud is still alive!"

BROOKS: "Good eye, Sprout."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Dominant bubble space for Sprout; smaller secondary area for Brooks.

## Scientific Visual Requirement

The image must still show surrounding damaged foliage. Do not depict
the entire branch suddenly green.

## Avoid

-   magical glow, plant suddenly healed, new leaves exploding from the
    bud;
-   Brooks holding or discovering the bud first;
-   celebration so exaggerated the scientific clue becomes secondary.

# PANEL 07 --- Understanding Winter Injury

## File

`S00E01-P07.png`

## Function

UNDERSTAND

## Camera

Medium two-shot -- pull back slightly from P06 to a wider framing than
any panel since P04.

## Composition

Brooks and Sprout, positioned so that more of the whole shrub is
visible than in P05/P06: both the damaged, exposed side established
since P03, and a healthier, more sheltered side of the same plant.
This is the panel where the "some tissue damaged, other tissue
alive" idea has to be visible, not just spoken -- frame the shrub so
both conditions read clearly in one shot. Leave open sky/fence
negative space above the characters for the three-line exchange.

## Brooks Pose

Gentle explanatory gesture toward the whole shrub -- not just the
bud -- notebook may be open, calm and thoughtful expression.

## Sprout Pose

Engaged, looking between Brooks and the plant, not a passive
listener.

## Scenery Detail

Same fence, same light direction established since P01. This is a
good panel to let a little more of the yard's depth show again after
five panels of tightening focus -- consistent with the visual rhythm
easing back out toward P08.

## Dialogue

BROOKS: "Winter can dry out exposed parts, even when some of the
plant is still alive."

SPROUT: "So it's not all dead?"

BROOKS: "Not all of it. That's why we look closely."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Generous -- this panel only carries the explanation, not the care
decision or closing caption, so give the three-line exchange clean
separated space without crowding the shrub detail that's doing the
visual half of the explaining.

## Scientific Visual Requirement

Both a damaged area and a healthier/sheltered area of the same shrub
must be visible in-frame.

## Avoid

-   showing only the damaged side (loses the visual half of the
    explanation);
-   Brooks in a standing lecture pose;
-   the shrub reading as fully healthy.

# PANEL 08 --- Look Before You Decide

## File

`S00E01-P08.png`

## Function

CARE / CLOSE

## Camera

Medium-wide closing shot -- pull back further than P07, echoing P01's
environmental framing without literally repeating it.

## Composition

Brooks and Sprout are together near the shrub, with enough of the
yard visible again (fence, a hint of the path) to bookend the
episode's opening image. The scene should feel calmer than P06/P07.
The shrub still visibly contains the same brown/tan winter-damaged
foliage established since P03. The viable area discovered in P05/P06
remains subtle within the plant, not spotlighted.

## Brooks Pose

Near Sprout rather than a dominant standing authority pose, gentle
expression, notebook may be closed or relaxed.

## Sprout Pose

Thoughtful, looking at the shrub with new understanding, loupe
relaxed in hand, active rather than passive.

## Dialogue

SPROUT: "So we shouldn't give up on it yet."

BROOKS: "Exactly. Let's watch what grows before we decide what to
cut."

## Closing Caption

"LOOK CLOSER BEFORE YOU DECIDE."

## Dialogue-Safe Area

Second-largest text allowance in the episode (after P01's none).
Reserve a clean upper region and/or separated left/right spaces for
the two-line exchange plus the closing caption, visually distinct
from character dialogue. If layout becomes crowded, that is a
signal to shorten dialogue during production review -- not to shrink
characters or typography.

## Scientific Visual Requirement

Brown damaged foliage remains brown. No instant recovery.

## Avoid

-   pruning action already happening;
-   removed shrub;
-   fully recovered plant;
-   giant educational text block;
-   Brooks lecturing from a standing authority pose.

# Cross-Panel Position Logic

The yard maintains understandable geography across all eight panels:
shrub stays in the same fence-line location; the stepping-stone path
and house edge do not jump sides arbitrarily; light direction remains
stable from P01 through P08; the leftover-snow patches placed in P01
stay fixed; the shrub's damage pattern stays stable; characters do
not change clothing; the loupe does not change design; Brooks'
notebook does not randomly change form.

# Panel Independence Test

Every panel must pass two tests:

## Carousel Test

Does it connect naturally to the image before and after it?

## Standalone Test

If someone sees only this image, is there still one clear visual
idea?

Both matter for Instagram.
