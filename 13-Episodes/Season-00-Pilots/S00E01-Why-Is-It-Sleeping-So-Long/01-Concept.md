# S00E01 --- Why Is It Sleeping So Long?

## 01 --- Concept

> **Is it dead... or is something else happening?**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Episode Role

This is the first production pilot of Season 00.

Its purpose is not only to tell a complete Lawn With Care Universe
story, but also to test the production system under real episode
conditions.

The episode should validate:

-   Brooks visual consistency;
-   Sprout visual consistency;
-   Brooks + Sprout relative scale;
-   Relationship Canon in an actual story;
-   discovery-based learning;
-   visual continuity between separate generated panels;
-   dialogue density for mobile viewing;
-   scientific clarity;
-   controlled use of the Discovery Frame;
-   Instagram carousel production;
-   final text-overlay workflow.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Production Format --- Pilot Hypothesis

**Revision (2026-08-10):** the pilot now tests an **8-panel carousel**,
up from the original 6-panel hypothesis. The extra two panels give a
dedicated environment-establishing shot at the open (addressing
production feedback that the yard/season deserved more visual space)
and split what had been an overloaded closing panel into a dedicated
science-explanation beat and a dedicated care/closing beat. See
`03-Story-Blueprint.md` v2.0 for the full architecture and the
retrospective note in `08-Retrospective-Notes.md` for why the split
happened.

Each panel must be produced as an **independent image file**, not as one
AI-generated comic page that is later cropped.

### Primary Instagram Format

**1080 × 1350 px**

Aspect ratio:

**4:5 portrait**

This is the primary production master for every panel in S00E01.

Example final files:

``` text
S00E01-P01.png
S00E01-P02.png
S00E01-P03.png
S00E01-P04.png
S00E01-P05.png
S00E01-P06.png
S00E01-P07.png
S00E01-P08.png
```

Each image must work individually while maintaining visual continuity
with the complete carousel.

### Important Production Rule

Do not generate multiple panels inside one image.

Generate:

**ONE SCENE = ONE IMAGE = ONE PANEL**

This provides better control over:

-   character consistency;
-   composition;
-   cropping;
-   dialogue placement;
-   regeneration;
-   mobile readability;
-   social publishing.

A combined page may be created later as a secondary derivative.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Text-Safe Composition

**Revision (2026-08-10):** dialogue is now written into each panel's
first-draft generation prompt rather than added only in a later
compositing pass (see `06-Visual-Prompts.md`, "Text Rule"). Negative
space still matters --- it protects a correction pass if the model's
text rendering needs fixing --- so each panel should still reserve
useful space for its dialogue or captions.

Do not place essential faces, props, clues or environmental evidence
directly against every edge.

Keep critical visual information comfortably inside the composition.

Exact production-safe margins may be standardized after the pilot.

# Core Mystery

After winter, Sprout notices that plants around her are beginning to
show signs of spring.

But one plant does not seem to be waking up.

Its exposed branches look dry and lifeless.

Sprout wonders:

**Why is it sleeping so long?**

Her first concern is simple:

**Is it dead?**

Brooks does not answer immediately.

Instead, he invites her to investigate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Story Hook

The episode begins with contrast.

Spring appears to be returning.

There are small signs of life around the yard.

Sprout is excitedly observing them.

Then she finds the exception.

One plant still looks like winter.

That visual contradiction creates the mystery.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Central Question

**Is the plant dead, still dormant, or has winter affected it in another
way?**

The story should avoid treating appearance alone as sufficient evidence.

The audience should learn to look closer before reaching a conclusion.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Scientific Territory

The episode explores post-winter plant condition, with emphasis on the
idea that exposed plant tissue can suffer winter-related damage while
other parts of the plant remain alive.

The exact scientific explanation and terminology will be locked in:

`02-Learning-Objective.md`

before the script is finalized.

The concept stage should not overstate whether the observed condition is
specifically winter desiccation until the scientific objective defines
the evidence shown in the episode.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Initial Observation

Sprout notices that the plant looks different from nearby plants.

Possible visible clues:

-   dry-looking exposed branches;
-   lack of obvious new growth;
-   apparently lifeless tips;
-   differences between exposed and protected portions;
-   small buds or living tissue that are easy to overlook.

The final evidence set must be scientifically validated before
scripting.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Sprout's Initial Hypothesis

Sprout may initially think:

**Maybe it died during winter.**

This is a reasonable hypothesis.

It should not be treated as foolish.

The story then demonstrates why observation and evidence are needed
before accepting it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Brooks' Function

Brooks must not begin by explaining the answer.

His first narrative function is to slow down the conclusion.

A canonical Brooks response is conceptually:

**What do you notice?**

He encourages Sprout to:

-   compare;
-   inspect;
-   look closer;
-   separate appearance from evidence.

Brooks helps structure the investigation.

He does not own the discovery.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Sprout's Function

Sprout should make the key observation that changes the investigation.

Her magnifying loupe may help reveal a subtle sign that the plant is not
uniformly dead.

This gives her genuine discovery ownership.

The episode should visually communicate:

**Sprout looked closer --- and found something important.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Discovery Arc

The conceptual discovery flow is:

## Observe

Other plants show signs of spring.

One plant appears not to.

## Question

Why is it still "sleeping"?

## Hypothesis

Maybe winter killed it.

## Investigate

Sprout and Brooks inspect rather than assume.

## Evidence

They discover that the plant's condition is not uniform.

Some visible tissue may be damaged while another part still shows
evidence of life.

## Discover

Looking dead from a distance does not necessarily mean the entire plant
is dead.

## Understand

Winter conditions can affect plant tissue unevenly.

## Care

Observe before making an irreversible decision about a plant.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Emotional Arc

The episode should move through:

**Curiosity**

↓

**Concern**

↓

**Investigation**

↓

**Surprise**

↓

**Relief / Understanding**

↓

**Care**

Sprout should not be frightened.

The emotional tension comes from uncertainty, not danger.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Relationship Test

S00E01 must visibly test the Brooks + Sprout Relationship Canon.

The episode should include at least one moment where:

-   Sprout is closest to the clue;
-   Brooks gives her space to investigate;
-   Brooks is at or near her conversational level;
-   Sprout discovers evidence;
-   Brooks helps interpret rather than taking credit.

The audience should feel that they solved the mystery together.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Visual Environment

The environment should communicate early spring after a Calgary-style
winter without requiring explicit geographic exposition.

Useful visual cues may include:

-   cool early-spring light;
-   patches of remaining snow only if seasonally appropriate to the
    chosen scene;
-   damp soil;
-   muted lawn color transitioning toward green;
-   dormant and emerging vegetation together;
-   exposed woody branches;
-   subtle signs of spring.

The environment should feel natural, residential and recognizable within
the Lawn With Care Universe.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Visual Storytelling Principle

The mystery should be understandable even before dialogue is read.

A viewer should be able to infer:

1.  spring is returning;
2.  Sprout notices a plant that looks different;
3.  she investigates it;
4.  she finds evidence;
5.  Brooks helps her understand;
6.  they leave with a more careful view of the plant.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Discovery Frame --- Pilot Test

S00E01 may test the Discovery Frame as a visual device during the
close-observation moment.

Candidate use:

Sprout examines a subtle plant clue through her magnifying loupe.

The Discovery Frame may isolate or enlarge what she sees.

It should answer:

**What did Sprout notice that we could not easily see before?**

Do not use the Discovery Frame merely as decoration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Discovery Band

The Discovery Band may be visible as part of Sprout's canonical design.

S00E01 does not need to introduce a new permanent badge.

If a badge is explored as part of the pilot, it remains:

**PROPOSED / NON-CANON**

until separately approved.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Brand Presence

Lawn With Care branding should remain accurate and restrained.

If the official logo appears, use the official source asset.

Do not ask the image model to reconstruct the logo.

The story must work even without overt promotional branding.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Initial Eight-Panel Hypothesis

The exact panel breakdown lives in:

`05-Panel-Breakdown.md`

Current (v2.0) eight-panel hypothesis:

### Panel 1 --- The Yard Wakes Up

Pure environment-establishing shot: the yard caught between winter
and spring, before any character beat.

### Panel 2 --- Something Is Growing

Sprout observes a specific sign of new life. Wonder.

### Panel 3 --- The Exception

Sprout notices the plant that still appears lifeless.

Mystery:

**Why is it sleeping so long?**

### Panel 4 --- What Do You Notice?

Brooks joins the investigation and encourages observation rather than
giving the answer.

### Panel 5 --- Look Closer

Sprout investigates with her magnifying loupe.

Potential Discovery Frame moment.

### Panel 6 --- This Bud Is Alive!

Sprout finds the key clue showing that the plant's condition is more
complicated than "dead or alive."

### Panel 7 --- Understanding Winter Injury

Brooks connects the evidence to winter plant stress/damage, in its
own panel rather than sharing space with the closing beat.

### Panel 8 --- Look Before You Decide

Brooks and Sprout decide to wait and observe rather than act
immediately.

Closing principle:

**Look before you decide.**

This eight-panel sequence supersedes the original six-panel
hypothesis after the Season 00 retrospective flagged the old closing
panel as overloaded; see `03-Story-Blueprint.md` v2.0 for the full
rationale.

# Instagram Production Requirements

Each final panel should:

-   be exactly 4:5 portrait for the primary version;
-   target 1080 × 1350 px;
-   remain readable on a phone;
-   have one dominant visual idea;
-   preserve character model consistency;
-   reserve room for controlled dialogue;
-   avoid excessive background clutter;
-   maintain continuity with adjacent panels;
-   function as a standalone image when viewed outside the carousel.

**Revision (2026-08-10):** dialogue is now included in the first-draft
generation prompt for every panel rather than deferred entirely to a
later overlay step (see `06-Visual-Prompts.md`, "Text Rule"). AI text
rendering is still treated as unreliable for final typography --- a
controlled correction pass remains available before export --- but the
wording itself is locked from the first draft, not added afterward.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Continuity Requirements

Across all eight independently generated images, preserve:

## Brooks

-   face;
-   proportions;
-   campaign hat;
-   wardrobe;
-   badges;
-   notebook/pencil when used;
-   relative height.

## Sprout

-   face;
-   visual age;
-   hair;
-   green headband;
-   outfit;
-   Discovery Band;
-   magnifying loupe;
-   relative height.

## Environment

Preserve:

-   same plant;
-   same yard/location;
-   same general time of day;
-   compatible weather;
-   compatible light;
-   plant condition;
-   spatial logic.

The plant itself is a continuity asset and should not radically change
between panels.

# What This Pilot Is Testing

## Test 01 --- Separate Panel Generation

Can eight independent 4:5 images maintain enough continuity to feel
like one episode?

## Test 02 --- Character Consistency

Can Brooks and Sprout remain visually stable across separate
generations?

## Test 03 --- Relationship Canon

Can Brooks guide while Sprout retains discovery ownership?

## Test 04 --- Visual Science

Can the plant evidence be shown clearly enough that the image
contributes to learning?

## Test 05 --- Mobile Dialogue

How much dialogue can fit without competing with the artwork?

## Test 06 --- Discovery Frame

Does a close-observation device improve comprehension?

## Test 07 --- Production Corrections

Which elements create the most regeneration or compositing work?

# Success Criteria

The concept succeeds if:

-   the mystery is understandable quickly;
-   the scientific question arises naturally;
-   Sprout has agency;
-   Brooks does not simply deliver the answer;
-   visual evidence matters;
-   the ending changes how the audience looks at apparently
    dormant/damaged plants;
-   each panel can be produced independently in Instagram-ready
    dimensions;
-   the carousel feels visually continuous;
-   the episode provides useful production lessons for S00E02.
