# Sprout

# Identity

> "The smallest questions often lead to the biggest discoveries."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the permanent identity of Sprout.

It establishes who she is before defining how she looks, moves or speaks.

Everything created for Sprout must remain consistent with the principles defined here.

---

# Character Overview

Sprout is the child protagonist of the Lawn With Care Universe.

She represents the natural curiosity every child is born with.

She notices details that adults often overlook.

She is not knowledgeable because she has studied more.

She is insightful because she observes differently.

Her greatest gift is asking the question nobody else thought to ask.

# Core Concept

Sprout represents childhood curiosity at its purest.

She sees possibilities before explanations.

Patterns before conclusions.

Beauty before utility.

She reminds adults that wonder is a way of thinking.

Her questions move the story forward.

Brooks helps organize the investigation.

# Life Philosophy

Everything has a story.

Nothing in nature is ordinary.

Questions are treasures.

Looking carefully is an adventure.

Learning is exciting.

Mistakes are part of discovering.

---

# Personality

Sprout is naturally curious.

Gentle.

Optimistic.

Patient when observing.

Energetic when discovering.

She is not hyperactive.

Her energy comes from fascination rather than excitement.

She speaks softly when something captures her attention.

---

# Emotional Intelligence

Sprout notices emotions quickly.

She comforts others naturally.

She often senses disappointment before adults do.

She celebrates discoveries more than achievements.

She values participation over competition.

---

# Strengths

Exceptional observer.

Creative thinker.

Pattern recognition.

Empathy.

Optimism.

Persistence.

Imagination connected to reality.

Natural curiosity.

---

# Weaknesses

Can become distracted by unexpected discoveries.

Sometimes follows a clue before explaining where she is going.

Asks so many questions that investigations occasionally become longer than expected.

Often forgets time while exploring.

---

# Fears

Hurting nature accidentally.

Missing something important.

Someone saying that curiosity is silly.

Being told not to ask questions.

---

# Internal Conflict

Sprout worries that asking "too many questions" might bother adults.

Brooks constantly reassures her that questions are welcome.

This relationship helps build her confidence.

---

# External Conflict

Her challenges come from mysteries.

Unexpected observations.

Natural phenomena.

Changing seasons.

Misunderstandings.

She never seeks conflict with people.

---

# Relationship with Brooks

Brooks is her mentor.

But he is also her learning partner.

She trusts him deeply.

She knows he will never laugh at her questions.

Brooks helps organize her discoveries.

Sprout helps Brooks see familiar things with fresh eyes.

They learn from one another.

---

# Relationship with Nature

Nature is her playground.

Her classroom.

Her friend.

She never treats nature like a collection of objects.

She believes everything deserves attention.

---

# Relationship with Animals

Sprout never assumes an animal needs rescuing.

She observes first.

She asks Brooks before interfering.

She believes every animal is living its own story.

---

# Relationship with Children

Sprout represents every curious child.

She is not "smarter."

She is simply more attentive.

Children should see themselves in her.

---

# Daily Habits

Always carries her magnifying glass.

Collects interesting leaves.

Looks under rocks carefully.

Keeps tiny discoveries inside her notebook.

Counts bird songs.

Talks quietly while observing insects.

Smiles when she notices patterns.

---

# Signature Behaviors

Tilts her head when thinking.

Squats instead of bending over.

Looks very closely before speaking.

Touches plants gently.

Looks up whenever she hears birds.

Often says:

"Wait..."

before pointing out something interesting.
