# Brooks & Sprout

# Relationship Canon

> "Brooks shows the way.
> Sprout discovers it."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical relationship between Brooks and Sprout.

It establishes how they:

- interact;
- communicate;
- learn together;
- appear visually together;
- share discoveries;
- solve problems;
- behave within stories.

Individual character canon remains authoritative for each character.

This document governs what happens when Brooks and Sprout are together.

---

# Core Relationship

Brooks is Sprout's mentor.

Sprout is Brooks' discovery partner.

Their relationship is built on:

Respect.

Trust.

Curiosity.

Care.

Patience.

Discovery.

Teamwork.

Brooks has more experience.

Sprout brings a different way of seeing the world.

Neither makes the other less important.

---

# Fundamental Principle

Brooks guides.

Sprout discovers.

This is the central rule of their relationship.

Brooks should rarely provide an answer before Sprout has had an opportunity to observe, question and investigate.

---

# Brooks' Role

Brooks creates conditions for discovery.

He:

asks questions;

provides context;

encourages observation;

introduces tools;

protects when necessary;

helps organize evidence;

connects ideas;

shares experience.

He does not exist to demonstrate how much he knows.

His success occurs when Sprout discovers something herself.

---

# Sprout's Role

Sprout creates momentum through curiosity.

She:

notices;

asks;

explores;

investigates;

tests ideas;

makes connections;

finds unexpected details;

shares discoveries.

Her curiosity frequently drives the story forward.

She is not simply Brooks' assistant.

She is an active discovery partner.

---

# Equality of Curiosity

Experience is not the same as importance.

Brooks knows more because he has explored longer.

Sprout sometimes notices things Brooks does not.

This is important.

Brooks must be genuinely capable of learning from Sprout.

Their relationship is:

Mentor + learner.

But also:

Explorer + explorer.

---

# Discovery Ownership

Whenever possible, Sprout owns the central discovery.

Brooks may:

notice clues;

ask questions;

suggest where to look;

provide scientific context;

help test an idea.

But he should avoid announcing the solution before Sprout has participated in discovering it.

---

# The Discovery Pattern

Their most common interaction follows this sequence:

## 1. Notice

Sprout or Brooks notices something unusual.

## 2. Observe

They look carefully before making assumptions.

## 3. Question

Brooks often asks:

"What do you notice?"

Sprout begins forming ideas.

## 4. Investigate

They move closer.

They examine.

They compare.

They may use tools.

## 5. Think

Sprout proposes possibilities.

Brooks helps organize the evidence.

## 6. Discover

The answer emerges from observation.

## 7. Understand

Brooks helps connect the discovery to a larger idea.

## 8. Care

They ask:

"What should we do with what we learned?"

Discovery should frequently lead to responsible action.

---

# Teaching Rule

Brooks never turns a discovery into a lecture.

He prefers:

questions;

short explanations;

examples;

comparisons;

demonstrations;

shared observation.

When Brooks explains something, it should feel like the final connection between clues rather than the beginning of the investigation.

---

# Sprout's Questions

Sprout is allowed to ask:

simple questions;

unexpected questions;

funny questions;

difficult questions;

questions without immediate answers.

Her questions are never treated as foolish.

Brooks treats every sincere question with respect.

---

# "I Don't Know Yet"

Both characters are allowed to say:

"I don't know yet."

This is a positive statement in the Lawn With Care Universe.

It means:

there is something to discover.

---

# Mistakes

Sprout is allowed to make incorrect guesses.

Brooks does not immediately correct her.

Instead, he may ask:

"What makes you think that?"

"What evidence do we have?"

"How could we test it?"

"What else could explain this?"

Mistakes create opportunities for discovery.

---

# Brooks Can Be Wrong

Brooks is also allowed to make mistakes.

When evidence contradicts his idea, he changes his mind.

He never protects his authority at the expense of evidence.

Sprout may occasionally discover that Brooks' first hypothesis was incorrect.

Brooks should enjoy this.

---

# Emotional Safety

Sprout should feel safe asking Brooks anything.

Brooks never:

ridicules her;

dismisses her;

embarrasses her;

talks down to her;

uses fear to control her;

makes her feel unintelligent.

Curiosity requires emotional safety.

Brooks protects that space.

---

# Physical Safety

Brooks remains aware of genuine environmental risks.

When intervention is necessary, safety takes priority over teaching.

He may:

stop Sprout;

move her away;

protect her;

take control temporarily.

Once everyone is safe, he explains why.

Protection should never become overprotection.

Sprout remains capable and independent.

---

# Nature Comes First

Their adventures are not about defeating nature.

Nature is not the villain.

Nature is:

teacher;

mystery;

system;

home;

living community.

Brooks and Sprout investigate nature in order to understand it.

Understanding should lead to care.

---

# Scale

Canonical approximate heights:

Brooks:

**~188 cm**

Sprout:

**~130–135 cm**

Their relative scale should remain visually consistent.

Minor perspective adjustments are permitted.

Character proportions should not drift simply to simplify composition.

---

# Eye-Line Rule

Brooks should avoid constantly speaking down toward Sprout.

During meaningful conversations he frequently:

kneels;

crouches comfortably;

leans closer;

sits nearby;

positions himself at a more natural conversational level.

This creates visual equality without pretending they are physically the same height.

---

# At Her Level

Brooks kneeling beside Sprout is a recurring visual motif.

It communicates:

attention;

respect;

patience;

shared discovery.

He does not kneel because Sprout is incapable.

He kneels because the discovery is often closer to the ground.

---

# Side-by-Side Rule

Whenever possible during exploration:

Brooks and Sprout move side by side.

Avoid repeatedly positioning:

Brooks in front;

Sprout following behind.

Their silhouettes should communicate partnership.

---

# Personal Space

Brooks creates safe and supportive physical space.

He does not crowd Sprout.

He allows her to:

move forward;

inspect;

touch when appropriate;

use tools;

make discoveries.

When she finds something, Brooks often remains slightly behind or beside her.

---

# Discovery Spark

A recurring composition should show:

Sprout discovering something first.

Brooks observing her reaction.

This reinforces the central relationship:

Sprout's curiosity drives discovery.

Brooks helps connect the dots.

---

# Investigation Staging

Preferred investigation composition:

Sprout closest to the discovery.

Brooks beside or slightly behind her.

When examining something near the ground:

both may kneel.

Sprout may hold the loupe.

Brooks may hold his notebook.

Their attention should converge on the same subject.

---

# Walking Together

Their walking rhythm should feel:

comfortable;

natural;

unhurried;

observant.

They frequently talk while walking.

Either character may point something out.

Sprout does not always need to look toward Brooks.

She is often scanning the environment.

---

# Shared Silence

Not every interaction requires dialogue.

Brooks and Sprout are comfortable observing quietly together.

Shared silence can communicate:

trust;

attention;

wonder;

respect for nature.

---

# Humor

Their humor is gentle.

Sprout may surprise Brooks.

Brooks may laugh at himself.

Nature may create unexpected situations.

They never use humiliation as comedy.

Brooks never makes Sprout the joke.

Sprout may occasionally tease Brooks affectionately, but never disrespectfully.

---

# Conflict

Brooks and Sprout may disagree.

Disagreement should usually involve:

different hypotheses;

different observations;

different interpretations;

different approaches.

Conflict should not undermine their fundamental trust.

Evidence resolves most disagreements.

---

# Protection Without Domination

Brooks protects Sprout when necessary.

But he does not solve every difficulty for her.

Before helping, when safe, he gives her an opportunity to:

think;

try;

adapt;

ask for help.

Brooks supports capability rather than dependence.

---

# Celebration

When Sprout makes a discovery, Brooks celebrates her thinking.

He praises:

observation;

effort;

questions;

reasoning;

patience;

care.

Avoid praise based only on being "smart."

Preferred idea:

"You kept looking."

Not:

"You're a genius."

---

# Discovery Band

The Discovery Band may record Sprout's growth.

Brooks does not award badges simply for obedience.

Progress should represent:

learning;

observation;

care;

teamwork;

responsibility;

meaningful discovery.

Badges are recognition of growth.

Not prizes for pleasing Brooks.

---

# Brooks' Notebook and Sprout's Loupe

Their signature tools express complementary roles.

Brooks:

**Notebook**

Record.

Compare.

Remember.

Connect.

Sprout:

**Magnifying Loupe**

Look closer.

Notice.

Explore.

Discover.

Together they represent:

Observation + Understanding.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

In shared scenes, Brooks should not automatically receive visual dominance because he is taller.

Composition may deliberately place Sprout:

closer to camera;

at the center of discovery;

in stronger light;

holding the key object;

leading movement.

Physical size must not determine narrative importance.

---

# Camera Rules

Preferred shared framing:

Eye-level compositions.

Medium two-shots.

Ground-level investigation views.

Over-the-shoulder discovery views.

Wide environmental shots.

Side-by-side walking compositions.

Avoid repeatedly using:

Brooks hero shots;

extreme low angles on Brooks;

Sprout hidden behind Brooks;

compositions that make Sprout visually subordinate.

---

# Composition Principle

When the story is about Sprout's discovery:

**Focus on Sprout.**

When the story is about their relationship:

**Frame them together.**

When the story is about nature:

**Allow nature to dominate the frame.**

---

# Silhouette

Their combined silhouette should be immediately recognizable.

Brooks:

Tall.

Campaign hat.

Ranger silhouette.

Notebook.

Sprout:

Small explorer silhouette.

Curly hair.

Discovery Band.

Loupe.

The height contrast is intentional.

Their visual language should still feel balanced.

---

# Color Harmony

Brooks contributes:

Deep forest green.

Grounded earth tones.

Visual stability.

Sprout contributes:

Cream.

Warm green.

Golden hair.

Visual energy.

Together they should feel:

natural;

balanced;

friendly;

trustworthy.

---

# Relationship With the Audience

Brooks represents:

"Let's think about this."

Sprout represents:

"Let's look!"

Together they invite the audience to participate.

The audience should feel like a third explorer.

---

# Story Function

Brooks provides structure.

Sprout provides momentum.

Nature provides the mystery.

Evidence provides the answer.

Care provides the meaning.

---

# What Brooks Never Does to Sprout

Never humiliates her.

Never dismisses her questions.

Never steals her discoveries.

Never treats curiosity as annoying.

Never demands agreement without evidence.

Never uses authority as the answer.

Never makes her dependent on him.

Never discourages exploration without a genuine safety reason.

---

# What Sprout Never Becomes

Sprout is never merely:

a sidekick;

comic relief;

a passive student;

a child who exists only to ask exposition questions;

someone Brooks constantly rescues;

a miniature version of Brooks.

She has her own perspective.

Her curiosity matters.

---

# What They Always Do Together

Observe.

Ask.

Investigate.

Listen.

Think.

Learn.

Respect nature.

Share discoveries.

Care.

---

# Quick Relationship Rules

1. Brooks is the guide, not the hero.

2. Sprout's curiosity drives the story.

3. Brooks supports rather than dominates.

4. Sprout participates in solving the mystery.

5. Evidence matters more than authority.

6. Brooks frequently meets Sprout at her physical level.

7. Sprout may notice things Brooks misses.

8. Nature remains a protagonist.

9. Discovery leads toward care.

10. They solve problems together.

---

# Relationship Test

Before approving a Brooks & Sprout scene, ask:

Does Sprout have agency?

Does Brooks respect her thinking?

Is Brooks guiding rather than simply explaining?

Does Sprout participate in the discovery?

Could either character learn something?

Does their body language communicate trust?

Does the composition avoid unnecessary hierarchy?

Does nature matter to the scene?

Does evidence contribute to the conclusion?

Does the discovery encourage care?

If several answers are "No", revise the scene.

---

# Relationship Statement

Brooks does not walk ahead so Sprout can follow.

He walks beside her so they can notice different things.

---

# Official Relationship Principle

**Brooks shows the way.**

**Sprout discovers it.**

And sometimes...

**Sprout shows Brooks something he never noticed before.**
