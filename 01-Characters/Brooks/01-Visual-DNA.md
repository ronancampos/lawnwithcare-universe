# Brooks

# Visual DNA

> "A great character should be recognizable before anyone sees his face."

---

# Purpose

This document defines Brooks' permanent visual identity.

It establishes the visual elements that make him immediately recognizable across comics, books, animation, educational materials and AI-generated illustrations.

These rules exist to preserve consistency throughout the entire franchise.

---

# Character Role

Brooks is the visual symbol of the Yard Rangers.

He represents:

• Curiosity

• Knowledge

• Trust

• Kindness

• Leadership

Every visual decision should reinforce those values.

---

# Recognition Test

A successful Brooks illustration should remain recognizable when:

• viewed only as a silhouette

• viewed from behind

• drawn in monochrome

• simplified into an icon

• reduced to thumbnail size

If Brooks cannot be recognized under these conditions, the design should be revised.

---

# Visual Personality

Brooks should immediately communicate:

Friendly

Reliable

Patient

Capable

Approachable

Calm

Curious

Safe

Children should instinctively feel comfortable approaching him.

---

# Shape Language

Brooks is primarily constructed using:

Rounded rectangles.

Soft squares.

Gentle curves.

His shapes communicate stability rather than speed.

Avoid:

Sharp spikes.

Aggressive angles.

Extreme proportions.

---

# Silhouette

The silhouette is sacred.

Its defining elements are:

• Wide campaign ranger hat

• Strong but relaxed shoulders

• Open jacket silhouette

• Straight posture

• Field notebook

• Rounded work boots

These six elements should remain visible whenever possible.

---

# Head

Brooks' head is slightly larger than realistic.

This improves readability and emotional connection.

The proportions remain believable while preserving children's illustration appeal.

---

# Face

His face should communicate warmth before authority.

Characteristics:

Rounded jaw.

Soft cheeks.

Kind eyes.

Relaxed forehead.

Natural smile.

Nothing intimidating.

---

# Eyes

Medium-large.

Expressive.

Always attentive.

His eyes constantly appear to be observing rather than staring.

The audience should feel that Brooks notices details.

---

# Eyebrows

Rounded.

Expressive.

Used to communicate curiosity more often than surprise.

Slight eyebrow raises are one of Brooks' signature expressions.

---

# Nose

Simple.

Rounded.

Natural.

Never exaggerated.

---

# Mouth

Small relaxed smile.

Brooks rarely displays exaggerated laughter.

His smile communicates encouragement.

Not comedy.

---

# Ears

Visible.

Simple.

Proportional.

Never stylized.

---

# Hair

None visible.

None.

# Posture

Brooks stands upright.

Chest open.

Shoulders relaxed.

Neck aligned.

His posture communicates confidence without dominance.

---

# Movement

Brooks never rushes.

His movement feels deliberate.

Purposeful.

Economical.

Graceful.

He walks like someone who expects to discover something interesting.

---

# Signature Pose

One foot slightly forward.

Notebook in one hand.

Relaxed shoulders.

Head slightly inclined toward the object of observation.

This pose should become iconic.

---

# Gesture Language

Brooks frequently:

Points gently.

Kneels to observe.

Opens his notebook.

Touches his chin while thinking.

Adjusts his ranger hat.

Invites others to look before speaking.

Never points aggressively.

Never crosses his arms while teaching.

# Recognition Checklist

Before approving any illustration ask:

✓ Can Brooks be recognized by silhouette?

✓ Is the campaign hat clearly visible?

✓ Does he appear approachable?

✓ Does his posture communicate confidence?

✓ Is the official palette respected?

✓ Does the image reinforce curiosity?

✓ Would a child immediately trust him?

If any answer is "No", revise the illustration.

---

# Visual Statement

Brooks is not visually impressive because he is powerful.

He is visually memorable because he makes others feel safe enough to become curious.

---

# Official Visual Principle

The audience should recognize Brooks before noticing the details.

Recognition comes from consistency.

Consistency builds trust.
