# Brooks

# Dialogue

## Purpose

This document defines how Brooks speaks in scripted dialogue: what he says, when, and in what order. It consolidates rules already established in `00-Identity.md`, `14-Canon.md` and `01-Characters/Relationships/Brooks-Sprout/Relationship-Canon.md` so writers don't have to re-derive his voice from scattered sources for every episode.

For tone and verbal register (how he sounds), see `12-Voice.md`.

---

## Dialogue Principles

- Calm, concise, curious, respectful, question-driven. Never condescending.
- Brooks asks more questions than he answers.
- He explains only after the child protagonist (or the audience) has had a real chance to observe — explanation is the last step, not the first.
- "I don't know yet." is a complete, acceptable, encouraged line.
- Simplify vocabulary for a young audience. Never simplify the idea.

## Ordering Rule

Brooks' dialogue should follow the Discovery Pattern from the Relationship Canon: notice → observe → question → investigate → think → discover → understand → care. He should not skip ahead to "understand" before the discovery step has actually happened on the page.

## Signature Questions

(from `00-Identity.md` / `14-Canon.md`)

"What do you notice?"

"What do you think happened here?"

"What makes you say that?"

"What evidence do we have?"

"What else could explain this?"

## Responding to a Wrong Guess

Per `Relationship-Canon.md` ("Mistakes"), Brooks does not correct a wrong guess directly. He asks:

"What makes you think that?"

"What evidence do we have?"

"How could we test it?"

"What else could explain this?"

## What Brooks Never Says

- Never delivers the answer before the discovery has happened.
- Never uses sarcasm toward children.
- Never lectures — break exposition into exchanges, not paragraphs.
- Never praises intelligence alone ("You're a genius"). Prefers praising process ("You kept looking.").

## Approved Reference Lines — S00E01

First production dialogue approved for Brooks (`13-Episodes/Season-00-Pilots/S00E01-Why-Is-It-Sleeping-So-Long/04-Script.md`), reproduced here as reference examples of his voice in practice:

"Maybe. But let's not guess yet. What do you notice?"

"Good eye, Sprout."

"Winter can dry out exposed parts, even when some of the plant is still alive."

"Exactly. Let's watch what grows before we decide what to cut."

## Signature Quote

"Let's look a little closer."
