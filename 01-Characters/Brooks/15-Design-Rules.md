# Brooks

# Design Rules

> "Consistency is invisible until it is broken."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the permanent visual rules for Brooks.

These are implementation rules.

They complement the Visual DNA.

Visual DNA explains what Brooks represents.

Design Rules explain exactly how he must always be illustrated.

Whenever uncertainty exists, this document takes precedence over artistic preference.

---

# Core Design Philosophy

Every illustration should answer one question:

**"Would a child immediately trust Brooks?"**

If the answer is no, the design should be revised.

Brooks should never appear intimidating.

---

# Visual Priorities

Whenever Brooks appears, the visual hierarchy should reinforce:

1. Face
2. Hat
3. Badge
4. Hands
5. Notebook
6. Field Kit

The audience should naturally read the character in this order.

---

# Silhouette Rules

Brooks must always remain recognizable by silhouette.

Essential silhouette elements:

• Campaign hat

• Straight posture

• Relaxed shoulders

• Rounded boots

• Notebook

• Ranger jacket

These six elements define the character.

Never redesign them.

---

# Campaign Hat

The campaign hat is Brooks' primary visual identifier.

It must always:

Remain forest green (#0E2B1C)

Have the gold band (#C9A227)

Keep the same proportions

Have a slightly rounded crown

Maintain the same brim width

Never appear tactical.

Never become cowboy-like.

Never become military.

Never become oversized.

Never become fashionable.

It is functional.

Timeless.

Professional.

---

# Badge

The official badge is sacred.

Construction:

Cream background (#F5F4EE)

Gold border (#C9A227)

Green leaf (#6DB33F)

Small maple leaf pin (#D85A30)

Placement:

Left chest.

Always visible whenever possible.

Never redesign the symbol.

Never add text.

---

# Jacket

Forest Green (#0E2B1C)

Structured collar.

Practical outdoor fabric.

Simple stitching.

No camouflage.

No tactical pockets.

No military appearance.

No unnecessary decorations.

---

# Boots

Rounded.

Black (#111111).

Outdoor work style.

Comfortable.

Never military.

Never combat boots.

Never hiking boots with exaggerated tread.

---

# Pants

Neutral earth tone.

Straight cut.

Functional.

Never skinny.

Never baggy.

Never fashion-oriented.

---

# Hands

Hands are one of Brooks' teaching tools.

They should remain slightly larger than realistic.

Used to:

Point gently.

Hold the notebook.

Gesture naturally.

Invite observation.

Never point aggressively.

Never use clenched fists unless absolutely required by the story.

---

# Face

Brooks should always appear approachable.

Rounded facial structure.

Soft jawline.

Kind eyes.

Relaxed smile.

Avoid:

Harsh cheekbones.

Deep wrinkles.

Extreme expressions.

Angular features.

---

# Eyes

Medium-large.

Warm.

Curious.

Focused.

Never appear angry without strong narrative justification.

Never stare aggressively.

---

# Smile

Default expression:

Gentle smile.

Closed mouth or slight open-mouth smile.

Never exaggerated.

Never cartoonishly wide.

---

# Eyebrows

Primary emotional tool.

Usually slightly raised.

Frequently communicate curiosity.

Never permanently furrowed.

# Discovery Frame™

Whenever Brooks actively teaches observation, the Discovery Frame should be incorporated into the scene whenever appropriate.

Never overuse it.

Never place it randomly.

It is part of storytelling.

Not decoration.

Brooks may carry:

Notebook

Pencil

Field Kit

Magnifying loupe

Soil probe

Moisture rod

Clipboard

Plant samples

Small collection containers

Weapons

Power tools

Military equipment

Oversized backpacks

Modern gadgets as visual focus

Luxury accessories

---

# Posture

Always upright.

Relaxed.

Balanced.

Confident.

Never arrogant.

Never slouched.

Never chest-forward in a dominant pose.

---

# Gesture Rules

Preferred gestures:

Open palm.

Pointing with one finger.

Holding notebook.

Offering objects.

Kneeling.

Looking closely.

Avoid:

Crossed arms while teaching.

Hands in pockets during explanations.

Pointing at people.

Aggressive movements.

---

# Walking Style

Steady pace.

Long relaxed strides.

Observant.

He often looks around while walking.

Never rushes without narrative reason.

---

# Kneeling

Whenever Brooks examines nature closely:

He bends one knee.

Keeps balance.

Remains comfortable.

Never squats awkwardly.

This pose should become iconic.

---

# Relationship with Scale

Nature should always feel important.

Do not exaggerate Brooks' size.

Trees should feel tall.

Grass should occasionally reach knee height.

Flowers may occupy significant portions of the frame.

Nature shares protagonism.

---

# Camera Rules

Preferred:

Eye level.

Three-quarter view.

Ground perspective.

Medium close-up.

Over-the-shoulder during investigations.

Avoid:

Hero shots.

Extreme low angles.

Dutch angles.

Action movie framing.

---

# Lighting Rules

Preferred:

Morning.

Golden hour.

Overcast.

Soft forest light.

Avoid:

Neon lighting.

Harsh studio lighting.

High contrast noir.

Colored dramatic spotlights.

---

# Emotional Rules

Brooks' emotions remain grounded.

Joy:

Warm smile.

Relaxed eyes.

Soft posture.

Curiosity:

Raised eyebrows.

Head slightly tilted.

Notebook ready.

Concern:

Focused eyes.

Quiet expression.

Never panic.

---

# Things That Must Never Change

Campaign hat.

Official badge.

Leaf symbol.

Official palette.

Relaxed posture.

Notebook.

Calm smile.

Functional clothing.

Rounded silhouette.

Educational appearance.

---

# Things That Must Never Be Added

Tactical gear.

Military styling.

Visible weapons.

Luxury watches.

Fashion sunglasses.

Bright neon clothing.

Corporate logos.

Brand sponsorships other than Lawn With Care identity elements.

---

# AI Consistency Rules

Every generated image must preserve:

Same face.

Same proportions.

Same hat.

Same badge.

Same palette.

Same posture language.

Same emotional tone.

Same educational purpose.

---

# Design Checklist

Before approving any artwork ask:

✓ Is Brooks immediately recognizable?

✓ Is the campaign hat correct?

✓ Is the badge correct?

✓ Is the posture welcoming?

✓ Is the palette accurate?

✓ Does he feel like a mentor?

✓ Does he look ready to investigate?

If any answer is "No", revise the artwork.

---

# Design Statement

Brooks should never look like an action hero.

He should look like someone every child hopes to meet during a walk in nature.

---

# Official Design Principle

Design should communicate trust before expertise.

Trust creates curiosity.

Curiosity creates discovery.
