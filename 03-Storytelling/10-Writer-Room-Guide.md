# 🌱 Lawn With Care Studio

# Writer's Room Guide

> "We are not writing stories.
>
> We are creating reasons for children to look more carefully at the real world."

---

# Purpose

This document defines how stories are conceived, developed, reviewed and approved inside the Lawn With Care Studio.

It is not part of the fictional canon.

It is an internal production manual.

Every writer, illustrator, editor, educator and AI collaborator should understand and follow this workflow.

The goal is consistency.

Not uniformity.

---

# Our Mission

Every story should help readers notice something they had never truly noticed before.

If the audience finishes the story looking differently at the world around them...

the story succeeded.

---

# Our Core Philosophy

We do not create fantasy.

We reveal reality.

Nature already contains extraordinary stories.

Our work is to help readers notice them.

---

# Before Writing

Never begin with a lesson.

Never begin with a scientific concept.

Never begin with a moral.

Always begin with curiosity.

Ask first:

"What in nature deserves to be noticed?"

Only after answering this question should an episode begin.

---

# Story Development Pipeline

Every episode follows the same creative pipeline.

```
Observation

↓

Question

↓

Research

↓

Story Idea

↓

Discovery Design

↓

Scientific Validation

↓

Episode Outline

↓

Script

↓

Story Review

↓

Storyboard

↓

Illustration

↓

Educational Review

↓

Final Approval

↓

Publication
```

No stage should be skipped.

---

# Step 1

## Observation

Every episode begins with a real observation.

Never invent phenomena simply because they are interesting.

Examples

A damaged leaf.

Morning dew.

A robin gathering grass.

A dry patch of lawn.

A mushroom after rain.

Nature writes the first draft.

We simply adapt it.

---

# Step 2

## Curiosity Test

Every story idea must answer YES to all of the following:

Does it begin with a genuine observation?

Does it invite curiosity?

Could a child notice this in real life?

Does it encourage looking closer?

Would Brooks be interested?

Would Sprout notice it first?

If not,

reject the idea.

---

# Step 3

## Scientific Validation

Before writing dialogue:

Research.

Never assume.

Every natural phenomenon must be scientifically credible.

Scientific simplification is acceptable.

Scientific distortion is not.

If uncertainty exists:

consult experts,

or allow Brooks to say,

"We don't know yet."

---

# Step 4

## Discovery Design

Identify:

What is the mystery?

What is the clue?

What is the false assumption?

What evidence changes the investigation?

Where does the Discovery Frame™ appear?

What changes in the reader?

Only after answering these questions should scripting begin.

---

# Step 5

## Character Balance

Every episode must respect the core relationship.

Sprout begins discovery.

Brooks protects discovery.

Readers complete discovery.

Neither character should dominate.

---

# Step 6

## Educational Check

Ask:

What habit is this story strengthening?

Examples:

Observation

Patience

Questioning

Comparing

Listening

Respect

Stewardship

If the answer is merely "teaching facts",

rewrite the story.

---

# Step 7

## Emotional Check

Every story should evoke:

Wonder

↓

Curiosity

↓

Hope

↓

Joy

↓

Reflection

↓

Care

If these emotions are absent,

the story is incomplete.

---

# Step 8

## Script Review

Review every scene.

Ask:

Does dialogue move curiosity forward?

Could this explanation become a question instead?

Could this dialogue become visual storytelling?

Could silence communicate more?

Rewrite whenever possible.

---

# Step 9

## Illustration Review

Illustrations must reveal information before dialogue does.

Backgrounds are active.

Readers should discover clues visually.

Every page rewards attention.

---

# Step 10

## Discovery Challenge

Every episode ends with an invitation.

Never homework.

Never obligation.

Always curiosity.

Readers should feel excited to try something outdoors.

---

# The Writer's Checklist

Before submitting a script:

✓ Nature is the protagonist.

✓ Curiosity comes before explanation.

✓ Brooks never lectures.

✓ Sprout notices something important.

✓ Scientific integrity is preserved.

✓ Wonder is visible.

✓ Humor feels gentle.

✓ Hope remains.

✓ Discovery creates care.

✓ The ending inspires observation.

# The "Could This Happen Tomorrow?" Test

Ask:

Could a child experience this tomorrow?

If yes,

the story belongs in the Lawn With Care Universe.

If no,

reconsider.

The franchise celebrates the extraordinary hidden inside everyday life.

---

# The "Walk Outside" Test

After reading the script,

would someone naturally want to go outside?

Observe a tree?

Watch birds?

Look at the grass?

Notice clouds?

If not,

the episode has not fulfilled its purpose.

---

# Common Mistakes

Never create artificial drama.

Never invent villains.

Never solve mysteries too quickly.

Never overload the episode with facts.

Never turn Brooks into a teacher.

Never turn Sprout into a prodigy.

Never sacrifice curiosity for plot.

Never prioritize spectacle over observation.

---

# The Studio Rule

Whenever there is a choice between:

More action

or

More curiosity

Choose curiosity.

Always.

---

# The Brooks Test

Would Brooks say this?

Would Brooks ask instead?

If Brooks sounds like a lecturer,

rewrite.

---

# The Sprout Test

Would a real child notice this?

Would a real child ask this?

If Sprout sounds like an adult,

rewrite.

---

# The Reader Test

Can the reader participate?

If not,

find a way.

Readers are honorary Yard Rangers.

# Creative Principles

Every decision should strengthen at least one of these:

Wonder.

Curiosity.

Observation.

Scientific Thinking.

Respect.

Hope.

Stewardship.

If a scene strengthens none of them,

remove it.

---

# Final Approval Checklist

Before publication:

✓ Canon respected

✓ Scientific review complete

✓ Character review complete

✓ Emotional review complete

✓ Discovery Challenge included

✓ Visual storytelling reviewed

✓ Dialogue approved

✓ Educational objective achieved

✓ Reader participation encouraged

✓ Studio philosophy preserved

Only after all criteria are satisfied should the episode become official.
