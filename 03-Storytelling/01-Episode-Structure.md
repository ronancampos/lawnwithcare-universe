# 🌱 Lawn With Care Universe

# Episode Structure

> "Every episode begins with curiosity and ends with someone seeing the world differently."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the official narrative structure used by every story in the Lawn With Care Universe.

It provides a repeatable framework that ensures consistency across comics, illustrated books, animation, classroom materials and social media adaptations.

Stories may vary in setting, season and subject.

The narrative rhythm remains consistent.

---

# The Story Engine

Every episode follows the same emotional cycle.

Wonder

↓

Observation

↓

Question

↓

Investigation

↓

Understanding

↓

Care

↓

New Curiosity

The story never truly ends.

It opens the next discovery.

---

# The Three Pillars

Every episode balances three elements.

## Wonder

Created primarily by Sprout.

---

## Scientific Thinking

Guided primarily by Brooks.

---

## Emotional Connection

Created by Nature.

None of these three pillars should dominate the others.

---

# Episode Formula

Every story contains seven narrative movements.

---

# Scene 1

## Curiosity Hook

Purpose

Capture attention immediately.

Length

5–10% of the episode.

Lead Character

Sprout.

Nature.

Goal

Present something unusual.

Never explain it.

Examples

A strange footprint.

A flower blooming in an unexpected place.

Silent birds.

A damaged leaf.

A puddle that disappeared.

The audience should immediately think:

"What happened?"

---

# Scene 2

## Observation

Purpose

Slow the story down.

Lead Characters

Sprout and Brooks.

Goal

Observe carefully.

No conclusions.

Readers begin investigating with the characters.

Dialogue decreases.

Visual storytelling increases.

---

# Scene 3

## Question

Purpose

Transform curiosity into investigation.

Lead Character

Sprout.

Brooks responds with questions rather than answers.

Questions should remain open.

Good examples

"What changed?"

"What do you notice?"

"What makes this different?"

Avoid

Leading questions.

Premature explanations.

---

# Scene 4

## Investigation

Purpose

Gather evidence.

Lead Character

Brooks.

Supporting Character

Sprout.

Tools

Magnifying Glass

Discovery Journal

Field Kit

Discovery Frame™

Measurements

Comparison

Sketches

Readers should feel they are participating.

---

# Scene 5

## Understanding

Purpose

Connect evidence.

Lead Character

Brooks.

Brooks does not lecture.

He helps organize what everyone observed.

The explanation should emerge naturally.

Science serves the story.

Never interrupts it.

---

# Scene 6

## Caring Action

Purpose

Translate understanding into behavior.

Examples

Protect habitat.

Leave animals undisturbed.

Water a young tree.

Observe without interfering.

Respect seasonal changes.

Sometimes the correct action is simply:

Keep watching.

---

# Scene 7

## Reflection

Purpose

Close emotionally.

Lead Character

Usually Sprout.

Ending Elements

Quiet moment.

New observation.

Small smile.

A new unanswered question.

The audience should feel that another story has already begun.

---

# Reader Challenge

Whenever appropriate, conclude with a Discovery Challenge.

Characteristics

Safe.

Simple.

Accessible.

Repeatable.

Examples

Observe three kinds of leaves.

Watch one flower for a week.

Listen for birds before breakfast.

Compare sunny and shady grass.

Find moss after rain.

Challenges are invitations.

Never homework.

---

# Character Leadership

The narrative focus shifts naturally.

| Scene | Primary Lead |
|--------|--------------|
| Hook | Sprout |
| Observation | Both |
| Question | Sprout |
| Investigation | Brooks |
| Understanding | Brooks |
| Caring Action | Both |
| Reflection | Sprout + Reader |

Nature remains present in every scene.

# Narrative Rhythm

Stories should breathe.

Fast.

↓

Slow.

↓

Quiet.

↓

Focused.

↓

Warm.

↓

Reflective.

Avoid constant excitement.

Curiosity grows in calm moments.

---

# Dialogue Density

Recommended dialogue volume.

Scene 1

Very little.

Visual first.

---

Scene 2

Minimal.

Observation dominates.

---

Scene 3

Dialogue increases.

Questions only.

---

Scene 4

Moderate.

Investigation.

---

Scene 5

Moderate.

Understanding.

---

Scene 6

Very little.

Actions matter.

---

Scene 7

Minimal.

Silence is welcome.

---

# Discovery Moments™

Every episode should contain at least one Discovery Moment.

Characteristics

Time slows.

Camera moves closer.

Dialogue nearly disappears.

The Discovery Frame™ may appear.

The audience begins observing independently.

These moments define the emotional identity of the franchise.

---

# Visual Priority

Show.

↓

Observe.

↓

Question.

↓

Explain.

Never reverse this order.

Images should communicate before dialogue.

---

# Educational Rhythm

Children should naturally experience.

Curiosity

↓

Observation

↓

Reasoning

↓

Understanding

↓

Application

↓

Reflection

Education is embedded inside the story.

Never separated from it.

---

# Scientific Rules

Evidence always comes first.

When uncertainty exists,

characters acknowledge uncertainty.

Acceptable phrase

"We don't know yet."

This reinforces scientific thinking.

---

# Humor Placement

Humor naturally appears during:

Observation.

Unexpected discoveries.

Sprout's innocent remarks.

Brooks' gentle reactions.

Never interrupt a Discovery Moment.

Never reduce scientific credibility.

---

Every episode should gradually build.

Curiosity

↓

Interest

↓

Focus

↓

Wonder

↓

Understanding

↓

↓

Inspiration

Endings should never feel empty.

# Adaptation Rules

Longer versions may include:

Additional observations.

Secondary discoveries.

Extra humor.

Character moments.

The core seven-scene structure never changes.

---

# Continuity

Every episode should reference previous discoveries when appropriate.

Examples

Sprout's Discovery Band gains a new badge.

Brooks refers to an earlier journal entry.

A recurring location shows seasonal change.

Readers are rewarded for following the series.

---

# Story Checklist

Before approving an episode ask:

✓ Does the story begin with curiosity?

✓ Does Sprout initiate the discovery?

✓ Does Brooks guide instead of lecture?

✓ Is observation shown before explanation?

✓ Does Nature actively shape the story?

✓ Is there a Discovery Moment™?

✓ Does the ending inspire future observation?

✓ Would a child want to explore outdoors afterward?

If any answer is "No",

the episode is not ready.

---

# Episode Statement

Every episode should feel like taking a slow walk with someone who notices beautiful things most people walk past.

---

# Official Episode Principle

A successful episode does not end when the last page is turned.

It ends when the reader walks outside and starts looking more carefully.
